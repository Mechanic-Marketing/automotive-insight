PHEV servicing in Perth: what plug-in hybrid owners need to know

Why a plug-in hybrid asks more of a workshop than a regular hybrid, and what short electric trips quietly do to the petrol engine.

0482 097 182
PHEV

Automotive Insight  ·  Shenton Park, Perth  ·  18 September 2026  ·  Updated 30 September 2026

Plug-in hybrids have gone from a niche choice to a common sight in a very short time. Australians bought more than twice as many in 2025 as in 2024, and BYD sold about half of them. A plug-in hybrid is part petrol car and part EV, and its servicing has to look after both halves.

Key takeaways

  • A plug-in hybrid has a much bigger battery than a regular hybrid, plus a charging port.
  • Its service should cover the engine, the battery, the charging system and the brakes.
  • The Outlander PHEV, BYD's Shark 6 and Sealion 6, and Toyota's new RAV4 PHEV are all common here.
  • Battery warranties are long, but each brand sets its own conditions.
  • Driving mostly on electricity doesn't let the engine off the hook.

How a PHEV is different

A regular hybrid charges its own small battery as you drive, and it can only move short distances on electric power. A plug-in hybrid has a much larger battery that you charge from a power point or a charger, and it can cover tens of kilometres without the engine running at all. To give you an idea of scale, Toyota's new RAV4 PHEV, on sale here since mid-2026, has a 22.7 kWh battery. Mitsubishi's Eclipse Cross PHEV had 13.8 kWh.

More battery means more to look after. There's the pack itself, an onboard charger, the charging port and cable, and on many models a second port for DC fast charging.

What a PHEV service should include

The engine still gets the full treatment, with oil, filters and fluids on the manufacturer's schedule. Mitsubishi and Toyota both service their PHEVs every 12 months or 15,000 km.

Beyond that, we test the high-voltage battery's health and capacity. Doing this at every service builds up a record you can compare over time. We look over the charge port and its pins for signs of overheating, corrosion or damage, check the charging cable, make sure the car charges and stops properly, and scan for any stored charging faults. We also check that the car moves smoothly between electric and hybrid driving.

Brakes get a close look, too. Because the electric motor does so much of the slowing, discs can rust and calipers can stick. The brake fluid still absorbs moisture as it ages, so it's replaced on schedule no matter how new the pads look.

Models we see

The Mitsubishi Outlander PHEV has been sold here since 2014, so there are plenty of older examples on Perth roads. Its smaller sibling, the Eclipse Cross PHEV, was sold from 2021 until production finished at the end of 2024. BYD's Shark 6 ute and Sealion 6 SUV were the two best-selling plug-in hybrids of 2025. And Toyota finally joined in 2026 with the RAV4 PHEV. Whichever one you drive, the servicing principles below apply.

Battery warranty and keeping an eye on health

Plug-in hybrid battery warranties tend to be long, with conditions attached. Mitsubishi, for example, covers the drive battery for eight years or 160,000 km on PHEVs bought after 1 October 2020, while its longer 10-year vehicle warranty only applies with dealer servicing. Other brands write their own rules, and some include a minimum capacity the battery has to keep. Hang on to your service records and battery test results. If you ever need to make a claim, they're your evidence.

Signs worth acting on include electric range dropping noticeably, charging that stops before it's finished, or the engine kicking in when it used to stay quiet.

How you drive changes things

If you mostly drive on electricity, the engine can go weeks without properly warming up. Engine oil still breaks down with time, so stick to the 12-month service even if you've barely added any kilometres. If you mostly drive in hybrid mode, the engine does more work, and you'll reach the kilometre limit sooner.

FAQs

Is a PHEV service different from a hybrid service?

It covers the same ground, plus the charging system.

How often does a PHEV need servicing?

Check your logbook. Mitsubishi and Toyota plug-in hybrids are every 12 months or 15,000 km.

If my brakes barely wear, do I still need to change the brake fluid?

Yes. Brake fluid absorbs moisture over time, however much or little you use the brakes.

Will servicing my PHEV away from the dealer affect the warranty?

Your standard warranty stays in place under Australian Consumer Law. Some extended cover needs dealer servicing, so check your terms.

How do I know if my PHEV battery is struggling?

Watch for shrinking electric range, charging that cuts out early, or a hybrid or EV warning light.
