Hybrid servicing

Hybrid car servicing in Perth: what actually gets done differently

What a hybrid service covers beyond oil and filters, how often to book one, and the warning signs worth acting on early.

0482 097 182
HV

Automotive Insight  ·  Shenton Park, Perth  ·  10 July 2026  ·  Updated 30 September 2026

A lot of hybrids around Perth get serviced exactly like a petrol car. Oil and filter, a look underneath, a stamp in the book, done. Nothing wrong with any of that, but it only covers half the car. The battery pack, the electric motors and the cooling system that keeps them happy all get skipped, and those are the parts that cost real money when they go wrong.

Key takeaways

  • A hybrid needs everything a petrol car needs, and then some.
  • Most Toyota hybrids run on the same 12 month or 15,000 km schedule as the petrol models.
  • Hybrid brakes last longer but are more likely to rust and stick.
  • The small 12-volt battery can leave a hybrid stranded even when the big battery is full.
  • Battery health can be measured. You don't have to guess from the dashboard.

What a hybrid service adds

The engine side of a hybrid service looks familiar. Fresh oil, a new filter, fluids topped up or replaced, and the air filter and spark plugs done when the logbook says so.

The extra work is on the hybrid system. We test the high-voltage battery to see how much it can actually hold, and whether any cells are lagging behind. We inspect the inverter and battery cooling, which work hard through a Perth summer. The regenerative braking gets checked along with the brakes, and we scan for stored hybrid faults, including ones that haven't lit up a warning yet. Everything is done to the manufacturer's specification, and your logbook gets stamped. You can see the full list on our hybrid servicing page.

About that "CVT"

Toyota and Lexus hybrids are often described as having a CVT, and people assume it's the same belt-driven gearbox found in plenty of small cars. It isn't. The hybrid version uses a planetary gearset and two motor-generators, with no belt at all. It's a tough design and rarely gives trouble. It still has fluid, though, and that fluid gets checked against the manufacturer's schedule.

Why hybrid brakes need looking at

Most of the time, a hybrid slows itself down with its electric motor, which feeds energy back into the battery. The brake pads barely get used, so they can last a very long time.

That sounds like good news, and mostly it is. The downside is that brakes which hardly ever get hot tend to rust. Bendix, the Australian brake company, says corrosion from underuse can leave calipers seized, and it recommends a brake check on hybrids at least once a year, brake fluid included. So even if the pads look almost new, the brakes still get a proper look.

The little battery that stops the big one

Every hybrid has an ordinary 12-volt battery tucked away somewhere. It runs the computers that wake the hybrid system up. If it goes flat, the car won't switch to Ready, even with a full main battery.

This mostly catches out cars that sit around. If yours spends weeks in the garage, Toyota dealers suggest a half-hour drive once a week to keep things charged. We test the 12-volt battery every time a hybrid comes in.

How often a hybrid needs servicing

For most Toyota hybrids, including the Camry, RAV4 and Corolla Cross, it's every 12 months or 15,000 km. That's the same as the petrol versions. Other brands set their own schedules. Honda's CR-V Hybrid is due every 10,000 km, for example. Your logbook is the final word, and if you've lost track, give us a call and we'll look it up.

Signs your hybrid battery needs checking

The battery is the most expensive part of a hybrid, and the dashboard says very little about it until something is already wrong. Things to look out for include fuel use slowly creeping up, the engine running more often than it used to, a battery gauge that swings between full and empty, or a hybrid system warning.

Heat is the big enemy here. Many Toyota hybrids cool the battery with air drawn from the cabin, through a vent near the back seat. If that vent is clogged with dust or dog hair, the battery runs hot and the car quietly cuts back its own performance to protect it. Toyota's advice is to vacuum the vent (never blow it out with compressed air) and to do it more often if you drive on dusty roads.

Hybrids we look after

Camry, RAV4 and Corolla Cross hybrids come through our Shenton Park workshop all the time, along with Lexus hybrids, the Mitsubishi Outlander PHEV, and hybrids from Honda, Hyundai and Kia. If you're shopping for a used one rather than servicing your own, call us and we'll tell you what to check on that particular model.

Your warranty

Servicing your hybrid with an independent workshop doesn't void the standard warranty, provided the work follows the manufacturer's standards. Extended cover can be a different story. Toyota's hybrid battery warranty is five years, and it only stretches out to 10 years if the car has yearly battery health checks and logbook servicing at Toyota. If your car is young, that's worth thinking about before you move.

FAQs

Does a hybrid service cost more than a petrol service?

There's more to check, so it can take a little longer. You'll get a written estimate before we start, so there are no surprises.

How often should I service my hybrid?

Go by your logbook. Most Toyota hybrids are every 12 months or 15,000 km.

Do hybrid brakes need less maintenance?

The pads wear more slowly. The brakes still need checking every year, because rust and sticking calipers are more common on hybrids.

How can I tell if my hybrid battery is on the way out?

Rising fuel use, the engine running more than usual and hybrid warning lights are the usual signs. A battery test will tell you for sure.

Can any mechanic service a hybrid?

Most can change the oil. Working on the high-voltage side safely takes trained technicians, the right procedures and hybrid diagnostic gear.
