Fleet vehicle maintenance in Perth: running hybrid and electric fleets

What changes when a fleet goes hybrid or electric, how to schedule around uptime, and the records that protect resale.

08 6150 5820

Automotive Insight  ·  Shenton Park, Perth  ·  1 October 2026

Walk through most Perth business car parks now and the fleet looks different. There's a Camry Hybrid in the pool car bay, a plug-in SUV or two on salary packaging, maybe an EV parked next to the work utes. Fuel savings started it, and tax has played a part. Once those vehicles arrive, though, the servicing side of running a fleet changes too.

Key takeaways

  • Hybrid and electric fleet vehicles need battery checks on top of standard servicing.
  • Service intervals vary between makes, so one calendar for the whole fleet stops services slipping.
  • Itemised invoices and stamped logbooks make tax time, resale and warranty claims easier.
  • Plug-in hybrids lost their FBT exemption from 1 April 2025, apart from some existing arrangements.
  • A mixed fleet can be looked after in one place.

Why fleets are changing

Fuel is the obvious driver. In stop-start city work, a hybrid uses noticeably less than a petrol equivalent, and across a fleet that adds up quickly. Tax is the other. Eligible electric cars can be exempt from fringe benefits tax. Plug-in hybrids used to be as well, but that exemption ended on 1 April 2025 unless an existing arrangement qualifies. Your accountant can tell you where your vehicles stand.

What servicing hybrid and electric fleet cars involves

Brakes are the first thing that changes. With regenerative braking, pads last much longer, but pool cars that spend days sitting in the yard tend to get rusty discs and sticky calipers. A proper brake inspection belongs in every service.

The high-voltage battery is the next. A health check and capacity test gives you a real number for each vehicle. Track it over time and you'll see problems coming well before they strand a driver. It's also useful evidence of condition when a car is sold or a lease ends.

Heat matters as well. Hybrids and EVs have cooling circuits for their inverters and batteries, and those work hard through a Perth summer.

Hybrids still have an engine, so they still need oil, filters and fluids. EVs skip all that, but they're heavy, which is tough on tyres and suspension.

And finally, the high-voltage side needs trained technicians who follow the right safety procedures. It's a fair thing to ask any workshop about.

Keeping the fleet on a schedule

Different makes run on different intervals. Toyota hybrids are every 12 months or 15,000 km, a Honda CR-V Hybrid is every 12 months or 10,000 km, and a busy ute will reach its kilometre limit well before the year is out. A spreadsheet can only keep up for so long.

Our fleet maintenance program starts with a conversation about what you run, how far each vehicle travels and when you can spare it. From there we build a service calendar that rotates vehicles through, so you're never without half your fleet on the same day. Early drop-off times and several hoists keep things moving. You'll have one contact for bookings and billing, and there's no lock-in contract.

Records and compliance

After each round of services, you get one invoice itemised by vehicle, which makes costs easy to allocate. Every logbook gets stamped, keeping the manufacturer's standard warranty intact under Australian Consumer Law. For hybrids and EVs, file the battery test results with each vehicle's records.

Recalls are carried out by the dealer, so check each VIN at vehiclerecalls.gov.au from time to time. And if a vehicle needs a pre-registration inspection, we're a DOT Authorised Inspection Station, so that can be done while it's with us.

One workshop for the whole fleet

Few fleets are all one type. You might run European cars for management, Asian utes for the crew and a growing number of hybrids and EVs. Splitting them across a dealer, a European specialist and a general mechanic means three lots of bookings and three lots of paperwork.

We look after all of them in Shenton Park, from European makes to hybrids and EVs from Toyota, Hyundai, Kia, Mitsubishi, Honda and Polestar. Our hybrid servicing page explains what a hybrid service covers. EV drivers can use our onsite charger while their car is in, subject to availability when you book.

One thing to keep in mind: some extended warranties depend on dealer servicing. Toyota's extended hybrid battery cover and Mitsubishi's 10-year warranty are two examples, so it's worth checking vehicle by vehicle.

FAQs

Do we have to sign a contract?

No. There's no lock-in, and we can change the service calendar as your fleet changes.

Can you service European, Asian, hybrid and electric vehicles in the same fleet?

Yes. That's exactly what we're set up for.

How do you stop the whole fleet being off the road at once?

We stagger services on a rotating calendar, with early drop-offs and several hoists running at the same time.

Will servicing with you affect our vehicle warranties?

Not the standard warranties. Some extended cover depends on dealer servicing, so check each vehicle's terms.
