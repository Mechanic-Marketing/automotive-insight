What we see on BMWs

The four things that usually bring a BMW through our door

Most BMW trouble gives you a warning first. A good service is the moment to catch it, while it's still a part and not an engine.

Timing chain guides

N20 four-cylinder · 320i, 328i, X1, X3 and others, about 2011 to 2015

What you notice
A whine or rattle, often clearest between 1,500 and 2,500 rpm.
What we do
The plastic guides go brittle with age. Replacing them before one breaks costs a fraction of a failed chain.

Cooling system

Most models with electric water pumps

What you notice
A temperature gauge that creeps, a sweet coolant smell, or the fan roaring from a cold start.
What we do
Check the pump, thermostat housing, expansion tank and radiator end tanks. Perth summers age these plastics quickly, so we look at them every service.

Oil leaks

Six-cylinder engines especially

What you notice
A burning smell after a drive, or spots on the driveway.
What we do
Trace it, usually to the valve cover or oil filter housing gasket. The filter housing one matters: oil can reach the drive belt.

Warning lights and coding

Every current BMW

What you notice
A yellow light on the dash, or a service reminder that won't clear.
What we do
Read the fault codes with dealer-level BMW diagnostic equipment, find the cause, and code or register new parts like batteries so the car accepts them.

Why you can trust the work

What our credentials mean for your BMW

A licensed repair business

In WA, a business that repairs cars has to hold a motor vehicle repair licence, and the people doing the work must be certified. If something goes wrong, you have a regulator in your corner.

Dealer-level BMW diagnostics

Our equipment talks to your BMW's modules the way the dealer's does. That means real fault codes, service resets, and new parts coded to the car. A generic scanner can only guess at most of that.

The same repair information as dealers

Since 1 July 2022, car makers have had to sell service and repair information to independent workshops on fair terms. We work from the same specifications as the dealer.

Before you book

BMW questions we get asked

Will servicing my BMW with you void the new-car warranty?

No. Under Australian Consumer Law, an independent workshop can service your car and stamp the logbook without affecting the manufacturer's warranty, as long as the work follows BMW's specifications and uses parts of suitable quality. The one exception is an extended warranty you bought separately. Some of those require dealer servicing, so check the terms first.

My BMW tells me when it needs a service. Do you work with that?

Yes. BMW's condition-based servicing tracks what's due, and we read it, carry out the items it calls for, then reset it.

Which BMWs do you work on?

The 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 7 Series, and the X range of SUVs. If yours is something else, call us.

How much will it cost?

That depends on the model and what's due, so we quote it for your car. You get an estimate before we start. Diagnosis is charged, and you're under no obligation to go ahead with a repair until you've heard what we found and approved the price.

Can I wait, or should I leave the car?

It depends on the job. We'll tell you the timing when we confirm your booking.
