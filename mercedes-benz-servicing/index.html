What we see on Mercedes

Four things worth catching at service time

Mercedes-Benz cars are built to go a long way. The ones that do are the ones where small jobs got done at the right service, not the next one.

Air suspension

Airmatic on E-Class, S-Class, ML, GL and GLE
What you noticeOne corner sitting low after a night in the driveway, or the compressor running longer than it used to.
What we doFind the leak, usually a strut or an air line, and quote the repair. A compressor working overtime to cover a leak is the expensive part to lose.

Oil where it should not be

Four and six-cylinder petrol engines, roughly 2005 to 2015
What you noticeSpots on the driveway, a burning smell after a run, or an engine light with no obvious cause.
What we doCheck the camshaft adjuster solenoids and the oil filter housing seal. The solenoids can weep oil into the wiring plug, and that is a wiring repair if it is left.

The automatic that was "sealed for life"

7G-Tronic and most Mercedes automatics
What you noticeA shudder moving off, a harsh shift, or no record of a gearbox service in the book.
What we doChange the fluid and filter at the interval for your gearbox, commonly around 60,000 to 80,000 km. It is the job most often skipped after the dealer years.

Diesel injectors and AdBlue

OM651 and later diesels · C, E, GLC, GLE
What you noticeA diesel smell in the cabin, a tar-like build-up under the engine cover, or an AdBlue countdown on the dash.
What we doReseal injectors before the leak eats the cylinder head, and sort AdBlue faults with Mercedes-level diagnostics rather than guessing at sensors.

Before you book

Mercedes questions we get asked

Will servicing with you affect my new-car warranty?

No. Under Australian Consumer Law an independent workshop can service your car and stamp the logbook without affecting the manufacturer's warranty, as long as the work follows Mercedes' specifications and uses parts of suitable quality. An extended warranty bought separately may have its own rules, so check the terms.

The dash says Service A is due. What does that mean?

Mercedes alternates between a smaller A service and a larger B service, and the car works out which one is next. We read the indicator, do that service, and reset it.

Which Mercedes do you work on?

The A, B, C, E and S-Class, the CLA, GLA, GLB, GLC and GLE, and the EQ electric models. We do not take Vito or Sprinter vans.

Do you do the electric and hybrid ones?

Yes. Our technicians are trained on hybrid and electric vehicles, and we have done inspections and servicing on EQ models.

What will it cost?

It depends on the model and whether an A or B service is due, so we quote it for your car and put it in writing before we start. Diagnosis is charged, and you decide on any repair once you have heard what we found.

How long will you need the car?

It depends on the job. We confirm the timing when we book you in.
